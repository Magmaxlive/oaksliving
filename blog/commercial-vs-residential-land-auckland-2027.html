Commercial vs Residential Land in Auckland: Which is the Better Investment in 2027?

Comparing yields, zoning, lending, and long-term outlook for Auckland land buyers.

← Back to Blog
2 November 2026

You've got two listings open side by side. One is commercial land for sale in Auckland's south, close to motorway access and busy industrial neighbours. The other is a residential block in a growing suburb, with schools and new streets taking shape nearby.

Both look promising. But which one will still make sense five years from now?

Let's compare the two using current numbers, so your next land investment in Auckland is one you can stand behind.

What are you actually buying when you buy land?

Bare land doesn't pay you while it sits there. You cover rates, interest, and insurance, and the return comes when you build, lease, or sell.

A residential site earns through homes people buy or rent. A commercial site earns through businesses leasing warehouses, offices, or shops.

Why is commercial land in Auckland so hard to find?

According to realestate.co.nz, industrial and commercial land in Auckland was valued at an average of $1,190 per square metre over the year to March 2026. That's the highest in at least a decade. The average industrial site listed for sale also shrank to a record low of 1,864 square metres.

Rents are still rising too. JLL reported that prime Auckland warehouse rents rose to around $203 per square metre a year in the second quarter of 2026. Prime industrial yields, which compare yearly rent to a property's value, stayed unchanged at 5.25%.

There are softer spots, though. Industrial vacancy rose to 4.0% in the first half of 2026, and CBRE reported weaker retail rents earlier in the year.

How is the residential side looking?

Barfoot & Thompson's July 2026 suburb report put the average three-bedroom home across Auckland at just over $1 million, with a gross rental yield of about 3.6%. In Flat Bush, the yield was higher at around 4.35%.

Then there's Plan Change 120. In July 2026, Auckland Council backed a more intensive version of the plan, which would allow taller buildings near major bus routes, town centres, and train stations. Final decisions are expected in mid-2027.

If you're considering residential development in Auckland, follow this closely. Zoning decides how many homes a site can hold, and that affects what the land is worth.

How do lending, tax, and costs compare?

Lending

Banks usually ask for a bigger deposit on commercial and bare land than on a standard home loan.

Tax

The two-year bright-line test applies to residential land, including bare land where a home could be built. IRD excludes business premises and farmland, so commercial land may be treated differently. Check mixed-use zones with your accountant. Interest on residential rentals has been fully deductible again since 1 April 2025.

GST

It can apply to land deals in ways people don't expect. Get advice before you sign.

Interest rates

The Reserve Bank kept the Official Cash Rate (OCR) at 2.75% on 28 October 2026 but signalled that it's likely to rise. Leave room in your budget for higher interest rates.

So which one suits you better?

Want steady income over several years? Commercial leases often run much longer than residential tenancies, and tenants usually pay many of the outgoings. The catch is that an empty commercial property can take longer to fill.

Prefer demand that holds up in most markets? People always need somewhere to live, and residential land is usually easier to finance and sell.

Some investors hold both, or choose mixed-use communities where homes, shops, and services grow side by side.

What should you watch in 2027?

Keep an eye on three things: the final Plan Change 120 decisions, whether interest rates go up, and how much ready-to-build land becomes available in growing areas like south Auckland.
