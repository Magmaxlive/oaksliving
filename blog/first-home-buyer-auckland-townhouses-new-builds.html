First Home Buyer in Auckland? Your Guide to Affordable Townhouses and New Build Homes

Townhouses, new builds, low-deposit options and KiwiSaver — a practical guide for Auckland first home buyers.

← Back to Blog
26 October 2026

You've been saving for years. You know the open home routine by heart. Yet most Saturdays end the same way, with the house you loved selling for well over your budget.

If you're a first home buyer in Auckland, it can feel like the market is working against you. The good news is that a standalone house with a big backyard isn't the only way in. Modern townhouses and new build homes can help you buy your first home sooner, often with a smaller deposit than you might expect.

Why are more first home buyers choosing townhouses?

Price is a big part of it. In many Auckland suburbs, a new townhouse often costs less than a standalone house nearby. That gap could mean buying this year instead of waiting another five.

Townhouses also suit the way many of us live now. You get two or three bedrooms, a low-maintenance outdoor area and easy access to transport, shops, and work.

A couple who once thought they'd have to buy an older house an hour out of the city may find a townhouse much closer in. Shorter commute, less upkeep, and more time for life.

How is a new build different from an older home?

A new build is ready to live in from the day you get the keys. You're far less likely to be calling a plumber in your first week or saving up to replace old windows.

New homes are built to today's Building Code, so they're generally warmer, drier, and more energy efficient than older houses. Through an Auckland winter, you'll notice the difference, both in comfort and on your power bill.

New homes also come with legal protection. Under the Building Act, residential building work is covered by implied warranties for up to 10 years.

Can you buy a new build with a smaller deposit?

In many cases, yes. Plenty of first-time buyers don't realise this.

The Reserve Bank's limits on low-deposit lending don't apply to new builds in the same way. As a result, some banks will lend on a new build with a 10% deposit instead of the usual 20%.

On a $750,000 townhouse, for example, you'd need $75,000 rather than $150,000. That's a much more realistic goal for most first home buyers in Auckland.

Every bank has its own lending criteria, so it's worth speaking to a mortgage adviser early.

What support is available for first home buyers in NZ?

There are two main schemes that can help first home buyers in NZ get onto the property ladder.

  • KiwiSaver withdrawal: if you've been a member for at least three years and haven't owned a home before, you may be able to use most of your savings for your deposit. You'll need to leave $1,000 in your account.
  • First Home Loan: this scheme is backed by Kāinga Ora and offered through participating banks. It lets eligible buyers purchase with a deposit as low as 5%. Income caps apply, and these can change, so check the latest criteria on the Kāinga Ora website before you apply.

The First Home Grant closed in May 2024, so leave it out of your budget.

What should you check before you buy?

A few simple questions can save you a lot of stress later.

  • Is the home already built, or are you buying off the plans? Buying off the plans can secure a price early, but you'll wait longer to move in.
  • Are there body corporate fees? If so, how much are they?
  • Who is the developer, and what have they built before?
  • Has the code compliance certificate been issued? If not, when is it expected?

Spend some time in the neighbourhood too. Visit on a weekday morning and again at the weekend. Try the commute at peak time, not on a quiet Sunday afternoon.
