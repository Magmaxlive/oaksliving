The Ultimate First Home Buyer Guide Auckland Buyers Need in 2027

Deposits, KiwiSaver, auctions, new builds — everything to know before your first home.

← Back to Blog
18 September 2026

If you're planning to buy your first home in 2027, that's closer than it feels. Most buyers start looking six to twelve months ahead, so the groundwork really starts now.

Between scrolling through Trade Me at midnight and doing sums on the back of an envelope, most first home buyers NZ wide hit the same wall. It all feels like a lot. Deposits, KiwiSaver rules, LVR restrictions, auctions, and a market that seems to change its mind every few months.

If you're searching for a proper first home buyers guide for New Zealand, particularly Auckland, you're not behind. You're just at the stage everyone reaches, ready to actually understand the process instead of hearing about it secondhand.

Let's go through it properly.

How Much Deposit Do You Actually Need?

The standard answer is 20 percent. On an Auckland home sitting around the $900,000 mark, that's roughly $180,000 sitting in the bank before most lenders take you seriously.

Here's where it gets more interesting. If you're a first home buyer, Kāinga Ora's First Home Loan lets you buy with as little as 5 percent down. You just need to meet the income caps. Right now, that's $95,000 for a single buyer, or $150,000 combined for a couple.

New builds also sit outside the usual deposit restrictions. That's one reason so many first home buyers end up looking at newly built townhouses and terraced homes, instead of older existing properties.

One more thing worth clearing up. The old First Home Grant closed back in May 2024. If you've read an older blog mentioning it, that information is out of date. KiwiSaver withdrawal, on the other hand, is still fully available. It remains one of the simplest ways to boost a deposit, if you've been contributing for three years or more.

What Does the Market Look Like for a First Home Buyer in Auckland?

Auckland is still the country's most expensive city to buy in. But it's not the frantic market it was back in 2021. Prices have settled, listings have grown, and buyers generally have more breathing room than they did a few years ago.

Suburb choice also matters here. Right now, several Auckland growth areas are seeing strong activity from first home buyers. Flat Bush, Hobsonville Point, Papakura, Karaka, and Westgate are among them. These suburbs have a steady supply of new townhouses and terraced homes, many built specifically for entry-level buyers.

More than half of the new homes built in Auckland over the past year were townhouses, not standalone ones. For first home buyers, that's exactly where the opportunities are.

What Should You Expect at an Auction?

A lot of first home buyers don't realise how different an auction is until they're in one. Once you win the bid, the sale is final, with no room to add conditions afterwards. Your finance needs to be fully approved, your building inspection done, and your LIM report reviewed, all before you raise your hand.

It sounds intense, but knowing what to expect makes it manageable. The buyers who struggle are the ones who assume they can sort the details out afterwards.

What's Changing for First Home Buyers?

There's genuine policy movement worth tracking if you're planning ahead to 2027. One proposal would lift the First Home Loan income cap significantly, potentially to $300,000. Another would apply that same cap to everyone, instead of a lower limit for single buyers and a higher one for couples.

Nothing has been confirmed as law. Treat this as something worth watching, not something to plan around just yet. If it does go ahead, more buyers would qualify for that 5 percent deposit pathway, right around the time many 2027 buyers get serious.
